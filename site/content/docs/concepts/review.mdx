title: Review
description: What a match will not decide is kept as a review item for a person or an agent, and a match can leave its whole changeset as a draft to approve.
---

A match decides the cases its rules settle and leaves the rest. What it leaves is recorded, so the question outlives the run and someone can answer it later. There are two things to review: **review items**, which are questions about records, and **drafts**, which are changesets a run was told not to commit.

This page raises one review item of each kind, answers each, and approves a draft.

## Before you start

The examples use the sample project with both of its files of rows loaded and matched, which is where [Getting started](/docs/getting-started) ends. If you have that project and its server is running, use it. If not, this makes it:

{/* sample: new session */}

```sh
spool init companies --example companies
cd companies
```

{/* sample: background */}

```sh
spool dev
```

Leave that running. In a second terminal, in the `companies` directory:

```sh
spool load samples/crm-1.csv
spool match
spool load samples/crm-2.csv
spool match
```

## The three kinds of item

| Kind | The question | Raised when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `possible_merge` | Are these identities one thing? If not, which of them does the record belong to? | A new record matches members of two or more identities. |
| `conflict` | Does this record belong here or not? | A new record matches one member of an identity and a no_match rule holds against another, or new records match each other and a no_match rule holds between them. |
| `possible_split` | Are these records still one thing? | A row is taken back, and nothing is left that joins the identity's other records. |

A record in an open `possible_merge` or `conflict` has no identity. An identity in an open `possible_split` stays as it is. Nothing is merged or split without someone deciding.

## A record that fits two identities

## Decide it

A reviewer who knows that `c-13` is the British company places it there with a changeset of one `assign`:

```sh
RECORD=$(spool show records crm:c-13 | awk '/^record/ {print $2}')
HALDEN=$(spool show records crm:c-5 | awk '/^identity/ {print $2}')
curl -s -X POST http://127.0.0.1:7765/v1/changesets -d '{"reason": "its invoices go to Leeds", "ops": [{"op": "assign", "record": "'$RECORD'", "from": null, "to": "'$HALDEN'"}]}'
```

```text title="Output"
{"seq":3,"changeset":"01a1f0a8-23cd-735e-89c5-270748ed3dd2","content_hash":"3ceebd766a3780fc4c9a5143142ce86ac9e3833bd0a429e600b622a4475ce2dc"}
```

```sh
spool show review
```

```text title="Output"
ITEM  KIND            STATUS    TYPE     RAISED BY  ABOUT
1     possible_merge  resolved  company  run 9      1 record without an identity, which matched 2 identities
```

The item closed by itself. A `possible_merge` or a `conflict` is open while any of its records has no identity, and any changeset that places the last one resolves it.

An item closes in one of three ways:

- **Resolved.** A changeset answered it. A reviewer may have written it, or a later match.
- **Superseded.** A later run looked again and raised an item that differs, or found that the question no longer stands.
- **Dismissed.** A reviewer closed it and left things as they are.

Open items are looked at again by every later match of their entity type. If the reviewer had fixed a rule so that the record matched only one identity, the next match would have placed the record and resolved the item.

## What each answer does

A `possible_merge` or a `conflict` is answered in one of these ways. The studio offers each, and says this before it is given.

| Answer | The records | The identities and their IDs | What is recorded | Later matches |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Place them in one identity | They join it | Every identity keeps its ID, and the others are unchanged | Only the `assign` | A match never moves a record that has an identity. Nothing keeps the identities apart, so a later record that matches both raises the question again |
| Merge the identities | They join the one that stands | The others are merged into it, and their IDs then answer with its ID | The `merge` and the `assign` | A match never splits an identity |
| A new identity | Each becomes an identity of its own | Unchanged | `create_identity` and `assign` | As for placing them |
| Dismiss | They keep no identity | Unchanged | Who dismissed it, when and why | No match looks at them again, a rematch included. A dismissed item is not opened again, and a changeset can still place them |
| Leave it open | They keep no identity | Unchanged | Nothing | Every match looks at them again |

The studio's merge keeps the identity with the most records. A merge is refused while a `cannot_link` is between records of the identities, and a record cannot be placed in an identity that one of its members has a `cannot_link` with.

`POST /v1/changesets/{seq}/undo` takes an answer back, with a changeset of its own:

- **A placement** is undone by moving the records out again. They wait with no identity, but the item stays resolved, so no match asks about them: only a rematch does, with `spool match --rematch`.
- **A merge** is undone by giving each merged identity its ID and its records back, and the records that were placed wait again, as above.
- **A new identity** is not taken back: its ID stays, with no records.

A `possible_split` has its own answers:

| Answer | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Split the identity | The larger side keeps the identity's ID, and the side that was not named keeps it on a tie. The other side becomes a new identity. With `keep_apart`, the default, one `cannot_link` between a record of each side is recorded, so no merge puts them back while it stands. Undone, the records go back and the `cannot_link` is removed; the new identity's ID stays, with no records |
| Say they are one thing | A `must_link` between a record of each group. The identity stays as it is. The `must_link` holds the groups together while the records it names stay in the identity: the question is not raised again when other records come and go, and it can be if a source takes back one of the records it names. Undone, the `must_link` is removed and the question is not raised again |
| Dismiss | The identity stays as it is, and nothing is recorded about it. The question comes back if another of its records is taken back, or one comes back |
| Leave it open | Nothing changes. No match looks at it, since its records have an identity. It closes by itself if the record that was taken back comes back, or a new record matches more than one group |

A merge or a split written against identities whose records changed since they were read is refused, and nothing is changed. A placement is refused if one of its records has an identity by then, and is not checked for anything else that changed since.

## Drafts

A match commits its changeset by default. Started with `"commit": false`, it stores the changeset as a **draft** and changes nothing. That is how a new rule, or an agent, is tried against shared identities without touching them. `spool match` has no option for it, so this match is started over HTTP:

```csv title="larkfield.csv"
id,name,country
c-16,Larkfield logistics,GB
```

```sh
spool load larkfield.csv --source crm
curl -s -X POST http://127.0.0.1:7765/v1/match -d '{"entity_type": "company", "commit": false}'
```

```sh
DRAFT=$(spool show drafts --status open | awk 'NR==2 {print $1}')
spool show drafts $DRAFT
```

```text title="Output"
draft            01a1f0a0-a840-778b-871c-76e67030b94b
type             company
status           open
from             match run 18, reconcile run 19
written against  log position 6
would place      1 record, 0 identities created

operations
  place record 5af84b72-c049-e1cc-0b28-2db7c0c0ef7d in 01a1efc6-41cd-775d-8758-e733aa086f7d
```

The draft is stored on the server, so whoever approves it need not be whoever started the match:

```sh
curl -s -X POST http://127.0.0.1:7765/v1/drafts/$DRAFT/approve
```

```text title="Output"
{"seq":7,"changeset":"01a1f0a0-a840-778b-871c-76e67030b94b","content_hash":"4f962e11c6cc1f736c03d3b041ea04ab03e887c0852120db38068a158cdb4396"}
```

```sh
spool show drafts
```

```text title="Output"
DRAFT                                 TYPE     STATUS     OPERATIONS  FROM
01a1f0a0-a840-778b-871c-76e67030b94b  company  committed  1           match run 18
```

Approving checks the draft like any changeset, against the identities as they are then. If they changed in a way that breaks it, the answer names the operations that no longer hold and the draft stays open. A draft can also be rejected, with `POST /v1/drafts/<draft>/reject`.

An entity type has at most one open draft. A later match of the type replaces it. The records of a draft that is rejected, replaced or never decided are examined again by the next match, so nothing is stranded.

## Recompute and what people decided

[`spool recompute`](/docs/concepts/matching#recompute) works out what the identities would be if every record were matched again by the rules as they are now. What people decided counts for more than the rules, and the output says which decisions shaped or stopped the result:

| Decision | What a recompute does with it |
| --- | --- |
| `must_link` | Its two records are in one identity, whatever the rules say. |
| `cannot_link` | Its two records are not. Where the rules join a group through other records and a `cannot_link` is inside it, the group waits for review without an identity, under a `conflict` item, and the output names the constraint and who recorded it. |
| Records a person or an agent placed, or identities they merged | They stay together, as the person put them, while what they joined is still whole. A rule's no_match between them is not read. Where the rules also join them with records that something says differ, they stay an identity and only those other records wait. |
| A split or a move with no `cannot_link` | Nothing says the records differ, so the rules may put them back together. The output marks every change to an identity a person or an agent changed, with the log positions. |
| A dismissed `possible_merge` or `conflict` | Its records stay without an identity. |
| A dismissed `possible_split` | The identity's records stay together. |
| An approved draft | It binds no more than the run that drafted it: approving accepted what the rules said then. The output marks every change that touches records an approved draft placed. |
| A rejected draft | Nothing. Its records were examined again by the next match anyway. |

Applying a recompute leaves its waiting groups as `conflict` items, closes the open items whose records it places, and replaces an open draft of the type.

## Who reviews

A reviewer is anyone the server knows: a person signed in to the studio, or a program with a token. Each change is recorded under its author.

An agent connects at `/mcp`. For Claude Code against `spool dev`: `claude mcp add --transport http spool http://127.0.0.1:7765/mcp`. It has tools to list review items, read what is needed to decide one, explain a pair, commit a changeset, split an identity, undo a change and dismiss an item. Every one of those that changes something requires a reason. [MCP](/docs/mcp) lists them.

## How many to expect

It depends on the rules. In the repository's benchmark on 5,000,000 voter records from five sources, the rules left 13,755 records for review. In its benchmark of data that changes, taking back 50,000 rows left 70 `possible_split` items.

## What review does not have

- Nothing notifies anyone that an item is waiting. Someone has to look.
- Items are not assigned to people, and there is no queue or priority beyond the list.
- The command line shows review items and drafts and cannot decide them.
- An agent cannot approve or reject a draft. There is no MCP tool for it.
