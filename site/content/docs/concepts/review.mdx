title: Review
description: What a match won't decide becomes a review item for a person or an agent, and a match can hold its whole changeset as a draft to approve.
---

A match settles what its rules can and records the rest, so the question outlives the run. There are two things to review: **review items**, which are questions about records, and **drafts**, which are changesets a run was told not to commit.

This page raises one review item of each kind, answers each, and approves a draft.

## Before you start

The examples pick up where [Getting started](/docs/getting-started) leaves off, with both CSV files loaded and matched. If that server's still running, use it. Otherwise:

{/* sample: new session */}

```sh
spool init companies --example companies
cd companies
```

{/* sample: background */}

```sh
spool dev
```

Leave it running. In a second terminal, in `companies`:

```sh
spool load samples/crm-1.csv
spool match
spool load samples/crm-2.csv
spool match
```

## The three kinds of item

| Kind | The question | Raised when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `possible_merge` | Are these identities one thing? If not, which of them does the record belong to? | A new record matches members of two or more identities. |
| `conflict` | Does this record belong here or not? | A new record matches one member of an identity and a no_match rule holds against another, or new records match each other and a no_match rule holds between them. |
| `possible_split` | Are these records still one thing? | A row is taken back, and nothing is left that joins the identity's other records. |

A record in an open `possible_merge` or `conflict` has no identity. An identity in an open `possible_split` stays as is. Nothing merges or splits until someone decides.

## A record that fits two identities

The sample has a Halden Group in GB and one in the US, kept apart by the country rule. Load a Halden Group with no country:

```csv title="halden.csv"
id,name
c-13,Halden Group
```

```sh
spool load halden.csv --source crm
spool match
```

placed      0 records, 0 identities created

```

```

The record matched both Halden Groups by name, and with no country the rule that separates them had nothing to compare. Joining either would be a guess, and joining both would merge two identities, which a match never does. So the record waits, and a `possible_merge` item says why.

## Decide it

A reviewer who knows `c-13` is the British company places it with a one-`assign` changeset:

```sh
RECORD=$(spool show records crm:c-13 | awk '/^record/ {print $2}')
HALDEN=$(spool show records crm:c-5 | awk '/^identity/ {print $2}')
curl -s -X POST http://127.0.0.1:7765/v1/changesets -d '{"reason": "its invoices go to Leeds", "ops": [{"op": "assign", "record": "'$RECORD'", "from": null, "to": "'$HALDEN'"}]}'
```

```text title="Output"
{"seq":3,"changeset":"01a1f0a8-23cd-735e-89c5-270748ed3dd2","content_hash":"3ceebd766a3780fc4c9a5143142ce86ac9e3833bd0a429e600b622a4475ce2dc"}
```

```sh
spool show review
```

```text title="Output"
ITEM  KIND            STATUS    TYPE     RAISED BY  ABOUT
1     possible_merge  resolved  company  run 9      1 record without an identity, which matched 2 identities
```

The item closed itself. A `possible_merge` or `conflict` stays open while any of its records is unplaced, and the changeset that places the last one resolves it.

Items close in one of three ways:

- **Resolved.** A changeset answered it, from a reviewer or a later match.
- **Superseded.** A later run raised a different item, or found the question no longer applies.
- **Dismissed.** A reviewer closed it without changes.

Every later match of the entity type re-examines open items. Had the reviewer fixed a rule so the record matched only one identity, the next match would have placed it and resolved the item.

## What each answer does

Answer a `possible_merge` or `conflict` in one of these ways. The studio offers each and explains it first.

| Answer | The records | The identities and their IDs | What is recorded | Later matches |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Place them in one identity | They join it | Every identity keeps its ID, and the others are unchanged | Only the `assign` | A match never moves a record that has an identity. Nothing keeps the identities apart, so a later record that matches both raises the question again |
| Merge the identities | They join the one that stands | The others are merged into it, and their IDs then answer with its ID | The `merge` and the `assign` | A match never splits an identity |
| A new identity | Each becomes an identity of its own | Unchanged | `create_identity` and `assign` | As for placing them |
| Dismiss | They keep no identity | Unchanged | Who dismissed it, when and why | Matches and rematches skip them. The item never reopens, though a changeset can still place them |
| Leave it open | They keep no identity | Unchanged | Nothing | Every match looks at them again |

The studio's merge keeps the identity with the most records. A merge is rejected while a `cannot_link` spans the identities, and a record can't be placed in an identity whose members it has a `cannot_link` with.

`POST /v1/changesets/{seq}/undo` reverses an answer with a changeset of its own:

- **Undoing a placement** moves the records out again. They wait unplaced, but the item stays resolved, so only `spool match --rematch` revisits them.
- **Undoing a merge** gives each merged identity its ID and records back, and the placed records wait again.
- **A new identity** isn't removed: its ID stays, with no records.

A `possible_split` has its own answers:

| Answer | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Split the identity | The larger side keeps the ID, and on a tie the side you didn't name keeps it. The other side becomes a new identity. With `keep_apart` (the default), a `cannot_link` between the sides stops any merge from rejoining them. Undoing it moves the records back and removes the `cannot_link`. The new identity's ID stays, with no records |
| Say they're one thing | Adds a `must_link` between a record of each group, and the identity stays as is. While the linked records stay, the question won't come back as other records come and go. It can if a source retracts one of the linked records. Undoing it removes the `must_link`, and the question doesn't return |
| Dismiss | The identity stays as it is, and nothing is recorded about it. The question comes back if another of its records is taken back, or one comes back |
| Leave it open | Nothing changes. No match looks at it, since its records have an identity. It closes by itself if the record that was taken back comes back, or a new record matches more than one group |

A merge or split written against identities whose records have changed since they were read is rejected, and nothing changes. A placement is rejected if one of its records has been placed in the meantime.

## Drafts

A match commits its changeset by default. With `"commit": false`, it stores the changeset as a **draft** and changes nothing. That's how you trial a new rule, or an agent, against shared identities safely. This match starts over HTTP, since `spool match` has no flag for it:

```csv title="larkfield.csv"
id,name,country
c-16,Larkfield logistics,GB
```

```sh
spool load larkfield.csv --source crm
curl -s -X POST http://127.0.0.1:7765/v1/match -d '{"entity_type": "company", "commit": false}'
```

```sh
DRAFT=$(spool show drafts --status open | awk 'NR==2 {print $1}')
spool show drafts $DRAFT
```

```text title="Output"
draft            01a1f0a0-a840-778b-871c-76e67030b94b
type             company
status           open
from             match run 18, reconcile run 19
written against  log position 6
would place      1 record, 0 identities created

operations
  place record 5af84b72-c049-e1cc-0b28-2db7c0c0ef7d in 01a1efc6-41cd-775d-8758-e733aa086f7d
```

The draft lives on the server, so someone else can approve it:

```sh
curl -s -X POST http://127.0.0.1:7765/v1/drafts/$DRAFT/approve
```

```text title="Output"
{"seq":7,"changeset":"01a1f0a0-a840-778b-871c-76e67030b94b","content_hash":"4f962e11c6cc1f736c03d3b041ea04ab03e887c0852120db38068a158cdb4396"}
```

```sh
spool show drafts
```

```text title="Output"
DRAFT                                 TYPE     STATUS     OPERATIONS  FROM
01a1f0a0-a840-778b-871c-76e67030b94b  company  committed  1           match run 18
```

Approval checks the draft like any changeset, against the current identities. If they've changed in a way that breaks it, the response names the failing operations and the draft stays open. Reject a draft with `POST /v1/drafts/<draft>/reject`.

Each entity type has at most one open draft, and a later match replaces it. Records of a rejected, replaced or undecided draft are re-examined by the next match, so nothing gets stranded.

## Recompute and what people decided

[`spool recompute`](/docs/concepts/matching#recompute) previews the identities the current rules would produce from scratch. Human decisions outrank the rules, and the output says which decisions shaped the result:

| Decision | What a recompute does with it |
| --- | --- |
| `must_link` | Its two records are in one identity, whatever the rules say. |
| `cannot_link` | Its two records aren't. If the rules join a group through other records and a `cannot_link` sits inside it, the group waits for review under a `conflict` item, and the output names the constraint and who recorded it. |
| Records a person or agent placed, or identities they merged | They stay together while what they joined is intact, and no_match rules between them are ignored. If the rules also join them with records known to differ, they stay an identity and only those other records wait. |
| A split or move with no `cannot_link` | Nothing says the records differ, so the rules may rejoin them. The output flags every change to an identity a person or agent touched, with log positions. |
| A dismissed `possible_merge` or `conflict` | Its records stay without an identity. |
| A dismissed `possible_split` | The identity's records stay together. |
| An approved draft | It binds no more than the run that drafted it: approving accepted what the rules said then. The output marks every change that touches records an approved draft placed. |
| A rejected draft | Nothing. Its records were examined again by the next match anyway. |

Applying a recompute raises `conflict` items for its waiting groups, closes open items whose records it places, and replaces the type's open draft.

## Who reviews

A reviewer is anyone the server knows: a person signed in to the studio, or a program with a token. Every change is recorded under its author.

Agents connect at `/mcp`. For Claude Code against `spool dev`: `claude mcp add --transport http spool http://127.0.0.1:7765/mcp`. Agents get tools to list review items, fetch everything needed to decide one, explain a pair, commit a changeset, split an identity, undo a change and dismiss an item. Every tool that changes something requires a reason. [MCP](/docs/mcp) lists them.

## How many to expect

It depends on the rules. In the benchmark on 5,000,000 voter records from five sources, the rules left 13,755 records (0.3%) for review. In the changing-data benchmark, retracting 50,000 rows raised 70 `possible_split` items.

## Where decisions happen

- The CLI lists review items and drafts. Decisions go through the studio, the HTTP API or MCP.
- Agents can decide review items. Approving or rejecting drafts is for people.
