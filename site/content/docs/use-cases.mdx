Spool fits when several sources name the same things, some of them in text, and other systems keep the ID. Each case below says what the data looks like, what Spool does with it, and a small example in Spool's own terms.

The examples were run by hand on version 0.1.0. Unlike the commands on the other pages, they are not in the samples check.

## Venture capital

This one is from Alex Patow, who wrote Spool.

I have built software for venture capital funds since 2020, and I wrote [Building for VC](https://buildingfor.vc), a guide for engineers who join a fund. I built Spool because mapping a huge number of data sources into one representation of each company, person and deal was one of the hardest problems I had there. Most datasets held some piece of the truth, and the complete picture came from several that overlapped. It was just as hard to reason about why two records had been merged, and to separate them when they should not have been.

**The mess.** A fund buys data about the same companies, people and investors from several providers, and each provider has its own IDs. Funding rounds from PitchBook, hiring from a people-data provider and the team's notes in the CRM can all name one company differently. Guess wrong and you double-count its funding, or miss that a partner already passed. Investors have it too: the CRM names the firm, one provider names its Fund II, another repeats the firm's name, and adding up the three inflates the totals.

Much of what a fund knows is text: research, meeting notes and memos, spread across documents and chat threads. A memo from last year names Acme Systems. The company is called Acme Robotics now, and whoever looks it up should find it and still see which name the memo used.

**In Spool's terms.**

- Each provider's export is a source, and each row is a record named by its source and key, so `crm:42` and `funding:42` are two records.
- A memo is a document. An extractor finds the companies, people and funds it names, and each place it finds is a record that keeps the characters it was found at.
- Rules on the domain and the name judge pairs. Each company is an identity, and its ID is what the CRM and the warehouse store.
- `spool explain` says why two records share an identity: which rule matched them, or who merged them and why. A wrong merge is undone, or split, and the split keeps the two sides apart from then on.
- A funding round can be an entity type too, so that two feeds' entries for one Series A are one round before anyone adds up the money.

**Example.** Three rows, one from each provider, and a memo:

```text
source   key  name                website
crm      42   Acme                acme-robotics.example
funding  42   Acme Robotics Inc.  https://www.acme-robotics.example/
people   319  Acme Systems        acmesystems.example
```

```text
Passed on Acme Systems in March. Come back to them when the second pilot is signed.
```

The website becomes a `domain` feature through the `domain_of` transform from [Entity types and features](/docs/concepts/entity-types-and-features#add-transforms). One rule matches on it, and the sample project's rule matches close names:

```sql
-- entities/company/rules/match/same_domain.sql
l.domain = r.domain
```

After the rows and the memo are matched, `spool show records` prints:

```text
source and key                 name                identity
crm:42                         Acme                01a1f134-c68e-7020-b3af-615f8b55ad1c
funding:42                     Acme Robotics Inc.  01a1f134-c68e-7020-b3af-615f8b55ad1c
memos:memo-2026-03.txt[10-22]  Acme Systems        01a1f134-c68f-7171-b3ec-d25a61125b0f
people:319                     Acme Systems        01a1f134-c68f-7171-b3ec-d25a61125b0f
```

The CRM row and the funding row share a domain. Acme Systems has another domain and a name too far from Acme, so it and the memo's mention are a second company. Someone who knows it is the former name merges the two, with that reason. The memo's mention now belongs to Acme Robotics, still at the characters that say Acme Systems, and the merge explains itself:

```text
`people:319` and `funding:42` belong to the same identity, 01a1f134-c68e-7020-b3af-615f8b55ad1c.
Run 4 (strategy `rules`) made them a candidate pair (block rule `name_prefix`) and gave no judgement.
...
`people:319` was placed at log position 3 by `user:01a1f134-c5cc-7462-bd8e-190729dee5e1` ("Acme Systems is the former name of Acme Robotics").
```

## Sales accounts and contacts

**The mess.** Two reps added the same account, as "Acorn Tools" and "Acorn Tools Ltd". Contacts arrive from email and the calendar with an address and no account. Call notes name accounts the CRM has under another spelling, or not at all. An enrichment tool adds a third spelling.

**With Spool.**

- The CRM's accounts and contacts are sources. An export of email or calendar contacts is another, with the domain of each address as a feature.
- Call notes are documents, and an extractor finds the companies they name.
- Rules match accounts by domain or by a close name, and keep apart those in different countries. Contacts match by email address.
- The account's identity ID goes in a field of the CRM. When two identities are merged, the old ID still answers with the one that took its place, and the [log](/docs/using-the-ids#follow-the-log) tells anything that follows it.

**Example.** The sample project of [Getting started](/docs/getting-started) is this case: nine CRM rows, and a call note.

```text
Call with Dana Reyes on 12 November. She has moved to Acorn Tools Ltd and runs
purchasing there. They now ship through Larkfield Logistics Ltd, and she asked
whether we work with Brightwater Foods Ltd, their new supplier.
```

Acorn Tools Ltd and Larkfield Logistics Ltd join the accounts the CRM rows made, which keep their IDs. Brightwater Foods Ltd is an account the CRM does not have, so it gets an identity of its own: a lead, found in a note.

An email domain is a good feature until it is `gmail.com`. A transform that gives no domain for an address anyone can get stops every Gmail contact from becoming one company:

```sql
-- transforms/email_domain.sql
lambda email: CASE WHEN lower(split_part(email, '@', 2)) IN ('gmail.com', 'outlook.com', 'yahoo.com')
  THEN NULL ELSE lower(split_part(email, '@', 2)) END
```

## Research and due diligence

**The mess.** The company you are looking at, and the people around it, are named in news, filings, interview notes and your own lists, each with its own spelling. Another company has the same name in another country. You want every document that names your company, and none that names the other one.

**With Spool.**

- Each text is a document. An extractor finds the companies and people it names, and each mention keeps its document and the characters it was found at.
- Mentions are matched against the companies you already hold as rows.
- A mention that fits two identities waits for review, with no identity, and joins neither by a guess.

**Example.** The sample project has two Halden Groups, one in GB and one in the US, kept apart by a rule on the country. A news item that starts "Halden Group has hired a new finance director" has no country to compare. The match leaves its mention out, and `spool show review 1` says why:

```text
review item  1
kind         possible_merge
status       open
type         company
raised by    reconcile run 8

record                                source and key          now
d3f71e28-892d-1a65-6247-1ba9c8962736  notes:news-1.txt[0-12]  waits for an identity

identities they matched
  01a1f135-1685-7241-975d-3bc8639b41aa
  01a1f135-1685-7241-975d-3bc909b3db16
```

A reviewer who reads the item assigns the mention to one of them, with a reason. [Review](/docs/concepts/review#decide-it) shows how.

## Support and customer records

**The mess.** The helpdesk knows a requester's email and an organization an agent typed. Billing knows accounts and invoice addresses. The product's database knows users and workspaces. "Which customers open the most tickets" needs one customer ID across the three.

**With Spool.**

- Each export is a source of one entity type, `customer`, with features such as the email domain, and a billing ID where a source has one.
- Rules match on the domain, and keep apart two different billing IDs.
- The warehouse joins tickets, invoices and usage on the identity ID. When someone merges two customers, the log says so, and nothing that stored the old ID is left pointing at nothing.

**Example.** A billing account and two tickets, with the `email_domain` transform from the sales case:

```text
source    key  name                     email
billing   b-7  Larkfield Logistics Ltd  accounts@larkfield.example
helpdesk  t-1  Larkfield                ops@larkfield.example
helpdesk  t-2  Larkfield                sam.k@gmail.com
```

```text
source and key  name                     identity
billing:b-7     Larkfield Logistics Ltd  01a1f135-5ae0-759f-a44c-2f17261ce140
helpdesk:t-1    Larkfield                01a1f135-5ae0-759f-a44c-2f17261ce140
helpdesk:t-2    Larkfield                01a1f135-5b53-7714-bbf0-581b5bbd86d9
```

`t-1` shares a domain with the account. `t-2` came from a Gmail address, so it has no domain, and an organization an agent typed is not enough to match on. It gets an identity of its own until someone who knows merges it.

## Compliance and KYC name matching

**The mess.** Customers' names have to be checked against a list: sanctions, politically exposed people, your own blocklist. Lists write names surname first, in capitals, with or without a middle name. Every hit is checked by a person, and "we checked, this is not the listed person" has to be kept with who decided and why.

**With Spool.**

- The customers and the list are two sources of one entity type, `person`.
- A transform puts the words of a name in order, so that "HOLT, Maren" and "Maren Holt" compare equal.
- A rule matches on the name, and a no_match rule keeps apart two different dates of birth.
- A customer that joins an identity with a list entry is a hit. A customer that fits two entries waits for review.
- The analyst's decision is a changeset with an author and a reason. A `cannot_link` constraint between a customer and an entry means no later changeset can put them in one identity.

**Example.** The transform and the rules:

```sql
-- transforms/name_words.sql
lambda name: array_to_string(list_sort(string_split(trim(regexp_replace(lower(name), '[^a-z]+', ' ', 'g')), ' ')), ' ')
```

```sql
-- entities/person/rules/match/same_name.sql
l.name_words = r.name_words
```

```sql
-- entities/person/rules/no_match/different_birth_date.sql
l.born <> r.born
```

The list has two entries for HOLT, Maren, born in 1971 and in 1980. One customer arrives with a date of birth, and the next day another with none:

```text
source and key  name         identity
customers:k-1   Maren Holt   01a1f136-777c-7294-a038-849ea835b87f
customers:k-2   Maren Holt   -
list:L-88       HOLT, Maren  01a1f136-777c-7294-a038-849ea835b87f
list:L-90       HOLT, Maren  01a1f136-777c-7294-a038-849dbed83c87
```

`k-1` has the date of birth of `L-88` and joined it: a hit. `k-2` matched both entries, so it waits in a `possible_merge` item. Without a date of birth, picking either would be a guess.

## Agent memory and knowledge bases

**The mess.** An agent remembers what it reads and hears: conversations, notes, wiki pages, tickets. "Dana", "Dana Reyes" and "the purchasing lead at Acorn" are one person, and "Acorn" and "ACORN TOOLS" are one company. A memory that stores each as written grows duplicates. One that merges them by guesswork mixes two people's facts, and nobody can say later why.

**With Spool.**

- What the agent reads is stored as documents, and an extractor finds what they name, with where.
- Mentions resolve to the same identities as the rows of your lists. The identity ID is the key the memory files facts under, or the tag on a retrieved chunk.
- An agent connected to [`/mcp`](/docs/mcp) looks identities up, asks why two records were matched with `explain_pair`, and corrects a wrong match with a merge, a split or an undo. Each correction is a changeset with its author and reason.

**Example.** In the sample project, an agent asked which notes name Acorn Tools needs two tools. `search_identities` finds the identity by a word in its records, and `get_identity` lists its five records. One is `notes:notes-1.txt[54-69]`: the note, and the characters that say "Acorn Tools Ltd".

## Catalogs

**The mess.** Several suppliers list the same songs, or products, each in its own way. One writes the album into the title, another puts the artist first. A length is "02:52" in one and "2.87" minutes in another, and a year is "1972" or "'72".

**With Spool.**

- Each supplier is a source, with transforms of its own that turn its columns into the same features: title, artist, album, track number, seconds, year.
- Rules compare the features, and a product's ID stays the same when next month's feed arrives.

**Example.** The repository's [`benchmarks/resolution/music`](https://github.com/alexpatow/spool/tree/main/benchmarks/resolution/music) does this to songs from MusicBrainz in five sources. These three rows of its sample are one song:

```text
title                                                        length   artist                         album                                   year
Moonlight Serenade (The Persuasive Trombone of Urbie Green)  02:52    Urbie Green And His Orchestra  The Persuasive Trombone of Urbie Green  1972
Urbie Green And His Orchestra - Moonlight Serenade           unknown                                 The Persuasive Trombone of Urbie Green  72
Moonlight Serenade - The Persuasive Trombone of Urbie Green  2.87     Urbie Green And His Orchestra                                          '72
```

On 193,750 records its rules ran in 8 seconds on four cores, at pair precision 0.997 and recall 0.862. Its first rules, "four of the six values agree", had precision 0.998 on 19,375 records and 0.157 on 1,937,500, where 2,532 different songs called "Intro" at track 1 became one identity. Measure rules at the size they will run at.
